# Behavioral Profile

## Overview
James's self-reported Birkman colour order is **Blue > Red > Green > Yellow**. The dominant Blue indicates an idea-first, reflective thinker who likes to understand a problem before acting; the strong secondary Red adds a practical, action-oriented drive to actually ship. Green (people/communication) and a lower Yellow (process/admin) round out the profile: he connects well with people and communicates deliberately, but is energized more by building and problem-solving than by heavy process or routine administration.

## Core Behavioral Drives

| Drive (Birkman) | Level | Meaning for James |
|-----------------|-------|-------------------|
| Blue (idea / reflective) | Highest | Thinks things through, wants to be informed before deciding, drawn to novel/interesting problems |
| Red (action / practical) | High | Bias toward building and shipping real, valuable things |
| Green (people / communication) | Moderate | Comfortable collaborating, strong at bridging perspectives |
| Yellow (process / structure) | Lowest | Less energized by rigid process and administrative routine |

## Strongest Behaviors
- **Clear, explicit communication:** Deliberately precise; repeatedly praised by coworkers for communication clarity.
- **Perspective-bridging:** Sees multiple sides of a problem and mediates when others struggle to understand each other.
- **Fast comprehension / AI advocacy:** Picks up new concepts quickly, aided by active use of AI tooling; an early adopter and internal advocate.
- **Value-driven builder:** Motivated by shipping things that create tangible value and make someone's day easier.

## How You Work Best
- Autonomy on meaningful work, with the context needed to make informed decisions
- Transparent teams and managers; strongly dislikes shady or opaque behavior
- Comfortable both collaborating and working solo; genuinely low-maintenance to work with
- Uninterrupted focus - being pulled off active work without a good reason is the main energy drain

## Growth Areas (frame positively in applications)
- **Dislikes unexplained context-switching:** Frame as a strong focus on delivery and follow-through - thrives when priorities are clear and stable, and pushes for the "why" behind a change.
- **Lower affinity for heavy process:** Frame as pragmatism and outcome-orientation - keeps process in service of shipping, not the other way around.

## Mapping to Job Posting Language

When a job posting mentions these keywords, it's a **strong behavioral fit**:
- "autonomy", "ownership", "end-to-end", "build from scratch / greenfield"
- "transparent", "open communication", "collaborative"
- "impact", "customer value", "solve real problems"
- "early adopter", "AI-forward", "experimentation"

When a job posting mentions these, flag as **potential friction** (not deal-breaker):
- "highly structured process", "rigid/waterfall", "heavy documentation/compliance overhead"
- "fast-paced, shifting priorities", "wear many hats / constant context-switching"
- "office-first culture" (especially downtown Toronto core)

## Management Style Preferences
- Works best under transparent, informed leadership that explains the "why" behind decisions
- Wants to be kept in the loop and given room to own his work
- Does not respond well to opacity, hidden agendas, or being redirected without reason

## Using This in Applications
- **Cover letters:** Weave in clear communication, perspective-bridging, and value-driven building - backed by the AI taxonomy project (impact) and Scrum Master role (communication/coordination).
- **CV:** Emphasize ownership of net-new systems, cross-team delivery, and measurable outcomes.
- **Interviews:** Use the taxonomy-matching and event-driven pipeline stories for problem-solving/impact; use Scrum Master and bridging examples for collaboration/communication questions.
- **Don't overstate:** He is not process/PM-driven by nature - don't position him as a heavyweight process owner or a people-manager (management is a future goal, not current experience).
